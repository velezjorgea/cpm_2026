Innovate UK: what changed this year

Innovate UK is not part of HE-BCI. These figures come from the UKRI release of Innovate UK funded projects, matched to the universities in the HESA data. Counts are projects with at least one core university taking part. Award values are the award offered to the lead participant, which is often a business, so they are not the universities' share. Years are Innovate UK competition years (April to March).

The series is restated and is not comparable with last year's release.

  • The Horizon Europe Guarantee scheme is included, as last year. Last year's file placed almost all of it (about 1,400 projects) in 2022. The current release spreads it across 2022, 2023 and 2024 (about 780, 510 and 190), which lowers 2022 and raises 2023 and 2024.
  • One large record in last year's 2023 figures (about £339m, the HVM Catapult programme) is not in the current release.
  • Award values were restated for some projects, and a number of project years moved. University names were matched again.
  • Values are now in real terms at 2024/25 prices. Last year's tables were in current prices.
  • Year labels now show the competition year itself. Earlier releases labelled each competition year one year earlier.
  • The latest year may be revised as late records are added.

Skills and talent: what changed this year

  • Graduates running their own business or professional practice now combines four Graduate Outcomes activities: running own business, self-employment or freelancing, developing a creative or professional portfolio, and multiple of these. Earlier editions counted running own business only. The new series is not comparable with earlier editions or with 2018/19, so it has no pre-Covid comparison.
  • Average learner days is the mean over all providers reporting in each year, as in earlier editions. The number reporting rose from 166 in 2018/19 to 223 in 2022/23, which pulls the average down: in 2024/25 it is 16.9% below 2018/19, whereas the 157 universities that reported in every year are 3.6% above. Total learner days also counts every reporting provider.
  • PhD graduates working in HE: the 2022/23 value is corrected from 42.0% to 36.0%, to count the Higher education industry only. The 2025 edition used the whole Education industry for that year.
  • Academic staff moving between HE and the private sector: the two series were labelled the wrong way round in the 2025 edition. Staff coming to HE from the private sector is the larger series, at about 2% of academic staff. The values are unchanged.
  • Students on sandwich courses is a new measure from 2022/23 and is not comparable with earlier years, so the series and its comparisons start in 2022/23.
  • Pre-Covid comparisons for employment, satisfaction and internship rates use 2018/19 values carried over from the 2025 edition, because the current Graduate Outcomes release starts in 2019/20.

Reading the numbers

  • The latest year differs by source. Graduate Outcomes measures and the graduate labour market measures end in 2023/24. Apprenticeships, learner days, academic staff measures and sandwich courses end in 2024/25. The Latest column shows the year used for each arrow.
  • The graduate labour market series (employment premiums and wage premium) is published by calendar year. Calendar year 2024 is shown as 2023/24.
  • Arrows use relative changes, a percentage of the earlier value, not percentage point changes. Within 1% is shown as stable and 10% or more as a double arrow.
  • The ten-year comparison needs ten years of data, so it is blank for measures whose series is shorter.
  • Apprenticeships and the graduate labour market measures are for England only. Academic staff measures, PhD graduates working in HE and sandwich courses are for the UK only.
  • Internship counts are small outside England, so nation figures can move sharply from year to year.

Reading the numbers

  • Money is in real terms at 2024/25 prices, using academic-year GDP deflators, so past years differ from earlier releases.
  • Total interactions and KS interactions differ. KS (knowledge services) interactions cover consultancy, contract research, and facilities and equipment. Total interactions also count licences granted, so they are higher. The headline table uses KS interactions. Both are in the data explorer, so you can see how much of a change comes from licences.
  • Growth rates are true five-year compound rates, from 2019/20 to 2024/25.
  • University of Liverpool SME consultancy reported before 2019/20 is moved to non-commercial, so the SME series is on one basis across 2019/20.
  • Innovate UK years are competition years (April to March) and are restated against last year's release. The Horizon Europe Guarantee is included.
  • Interaction counts fall across many universities in 2019/20, which is a change in reporting. Read comparisons across that year with care.
  • Series run from 2014/15 where the source has them. Innovate UK starts in 2016/17.
  • Use the switch under the tabs to change which universities are counted (see below).

Which universities are counted

All reporting universities is the default and matches the Research England workbook. It includes every university that reported to HE-BCI in each year. That number is not constant: 163 universities reported in 2014/15, 165 in 2018/19, 195 in 2019/20, a peak of 223 in 2022/23 and 206 in 2024/25. Some of the growth in totals therefore reflects wider reporting, not only changes in activity.

Continuously reporting keeps only the 157 universities that reported in all eleven years from 2014/15 to 2024/25, so every year is like-for-like. The State of the Relationship report uses this basis where it looks at how income is spread across universities and where it reports shares of collaboration income, and labels it wherever it is used. Totals and shares in the report can therefore differ slightly from the default view here.

Innovate UK figures are the same on both settings, because the Innovate UK data records the lead participant of each project, not the university, so it cannot be limited to a set of universities.

Downloads

Every table and chart has a download button. CSV files hold the figures as displayed on the page, in the units shown. For research and innovation the files follow the setting for universities counted, and "Download all data" gives both settings in one file. For skills and talent, "Download all data" gives every measure, nation and year. Chart downloads are PNG images without a title, so they can be captioned wherever they are used.
